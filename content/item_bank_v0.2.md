## G5-EN-0001 — Multi-digit multiplication

**Statement.** A school orders 24 boxes of exercise books. Each box holds 36 books. How many exercise books does the school order altogether?

**Topic:** Number and operations in base ten · **Difficulty:** 1 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Multiply a two-digit number by a two-digit number; Translate a word problem into a multiplication

**Prerequisite skills:** Times tables to 10 × 10; Place value to thousands; Column addition

**Canonical answer:** 864 exercise books  
**Also accepted:** 864, 864 books, 864 exercise books

**Solution steps.**

1. Equal groups: 24 groups of 36, so multiply 24 × 36.
2. Split 36 into 30 + 6.
3. 24 × 30 = 720.
4. 24 × 6 = 144.
5. 720 + 144 = 864.
6. The school orders 864 exercise books.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Every box has the same number of books. Does that suggest adding or multiplying?
- _Hint 2:_ You need 24 × 36. Try breaking 36 into 30 and 6 and multiplying each part by 24.
- _Hint 3:_ 24 × 30 = 720. Now work out 24 × 6, then add your two answers together.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                            | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adds instead of multiplies.                                              | 60                   | Ask what 24 and 36 each count — boxes and books-per-box — and whether adding them gives a number of books. |
| Forgets to shift the partial product when multiplying by the tens digit. | 216                  | Ask the value of the 3 in 36. Multiplying by 3 tens must give tens, not ones.                              |
| Adds partial products incorrectly.                                       | 854 or 964           | Ask the student to re-add 720 + 144 with the columns lined up.                                             |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Recognising the situation as 24 equal groups of 36. · Answer: Exact match to 864; unit 'books' expected but prompt for it rather than mark wrong. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0002 — Division with remainder interpretation

**Statement.** 197 children are going on a trip. Each minibus can carry 15 children. How many minibuses are needed so that every child has a seat?

**Topic:** Number and operations in base ten · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Divide a three-digit number by a two-digit number; Interpret a remainder in context (round up)

**Prerequisite skills:** Multiplication facts; Meaning of remainder; Estimation

**Canonical answer:** 14 minibuses  
**Also accepted:** 14, 14 minibuses, 14 buses

**Solution steps.**

1. Divide the children into groups of 15: 197 ÷ 15.
2. 15 × 13 = 195, and 197 − 195 = 2, so 197 ÷ 15 = 13 remainder 2.
3. 13 full minibuses carry 195 children; 2 children still have no seat.
4. Those 2 children still need a vehicle, so one more minibus is required.
5. 13 + 1 = 14 minibuses.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Start by finding how many full minibuses you can fill.
- _Hint 2:_ 197 ÷ 15 leaves a remainder. What happens to the children in that remainder?
- _Hint 3:_ 13 minibuses seat 195 children. Two children are left over — do they walk, or do they need a bus of their own?

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                           | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ignores the remainder.                  | 13                   | Ask how many children the 13 buses carry, and what happens to the other 2.         |
| Reports the raw division as the answer. | 13 r 2               | Ask what the question actually counts — buses, not leftovers.                      |
| Rounds the decimal down (13.13 → 13).   | 13                   | Point out that a part of a bus cannot be ordered; in seating problems we round up. |
| Division fact error.                    | 12 or 15             | Ask the student to check by multiplying their answer by 15.                        |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Rounding up because of the remainder. · Answer: Exact match to 14. '13 r 2' is incorrect for this question. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0003 — Adding fractions with unlike denominators

**Statement.** Maya read 1/3 of a book on Monday and 2/5 of the same book on Tuesday. What fraction of the book has she read altogether?

**Topic:** Fractions · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Find a common denominator; Add fractions with unlike denominators; Express the answer in simplest form

**Prerequisite skills:** Equivalent fractions; Multiples and lowest common multiple; Fraction as part of a whole

**Canonical answer:** 11/15 of the book  
**Also accepted:** 11/15, 11 / 15, eleven fifteenths, 0.7333... (only if the student is asked for a decimal)

**Solution steps.**

1. The two fractions have different denominators, so they must be rewritten with a common denominator.
2. The lowest common multiple of 3 and 5 is 15.
3. 1/3 = 5/15 and 2/5 = 6/15.
4. 5/15 + 6/15 = 11/15.
5. 11 and 15 share no common factor, so 11/15 is already in simplest form.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Can you add thirds and fifths directly, or do the pieces need to be the same size first?
- _Hint 2:_ Find a number that both 3 and 5 divide into, and rewrite both fractions with that denominator.
- _Hint 3:_ Use fifteenths: 1/3 = 5/15. What does 2/5 become? Then add only the numerators.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                   | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adds numerators and denominators.                               | 3/8                  | Ask whether 3/8 is more or less than 1/3 — the answer must be bigger than either part.             |
| Finds a common denominator but forgets to scale the numerators. | 3/15                 | Ask what you multiplied the denominator by, and remind them the numerator gets the same treatment. |
| Uses 8 or 15 as denominator with unconverted numerators.        | 3/15                 | Have the student draw or state 1/3 as fifteenths before adding.                                    |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Converting both fractions to fifteenths before adding. · Answer: 11/15 or any equivalent fraction (e.g. 22/30) — accept, but prompt for simplest form. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0004 — Subtracting mixed numbers with regrouping

**Statement.** A ribbon is 4 1/4 metres long. Sam cuts off 1 2/3 metres. How much ribbon is left?

**Topic:** Fractions · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Subtract mixed numbers with unlike denominators; Regroup a whole into fractional parts

**Prerequisite skills:** Equivalent fractions; Common denominators; Converting between mixed and improper fractions

**Canonical answer:** 2 7/12 metres  
**Also accepted:** 2 7/12, 2 7/12 m, 31/12, 31/12 m

**Solution steps.**

1. Rewrite both fractions with denominator 12: 4 1/4 = 4 3/12 and 1 2/3 = 1 8/12.
2. 3/12 is smaller than 8/12, so regroup one whole from the 4: 4 3/12 = 3 15/12.
3. Subtract the whole numbers: 3 − 1 = 2.
4. Subtract the fractions: 15/12 − 8/12 = 7/12.
5. The ribbon left is 2 7/12 metres.
6. Check: 2 7/12 + 1 8/12 = 3 15/12 = 4 3/12 = 4 1/4. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Quarters and thirds are different-sized pieces. What is the first thing to fix?
- _Hint 2:_ In twelfths the problem is 4 3/12 − 1 8/12. You cannot take 8 twelfths from 3 twelfths yet — what could you borrow?
- _Hint 3:_ Exchange one whole metre for 12/12. That turns 4 3/12 into 3 15/12. Now subtract the wholes and the twelfths separately.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                       | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Subtracts the smaller fraction from the larger regardless of order. | 3 5/12               | Ask which number is on top; 3/12 − 8/12 cannot give a positive result without regrouping.        |
| Subtracts numerators and denominators.                              | 3 1/1 or 3           | Return to what a denominator means — it names the size of the piece and does not get subtracted. |
| Regroups but adds only 10 (place-value habit) instead of 12.        | 2 5/12               | Ask how many twelfths make one whole.                                                            |
| Forgets to reduce the whole number after regrouping.                | 3 7/12               | Ask where the borrowed whole came from.                                                          |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Regrouping one whole as 12/12 before subtracting. · Answer: 2 7/12 or 31/12, with metres. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3, R4

## G5-EN-0005 — Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

**Statement.** One batch of pancakes needs 3/4 of a cup of milk. How much milk is needed for 3 batches? Give your answer as a mixed number.

**Topic:** Fractions · **Difficulty:** 1 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Multiply a fraction by a whole number; Convert an improper fraction to a mixed number

**Prerequisite skills:** Repeated addition; Meaning of numerator and denominator; Division facts

**Canonical answer:** 2 1/4 cups  
**Also accepted:** 2 1/4, 2 1/4 cups, 2.25 cups, 9/4 (prompt for mixed number as requested)

**Solution steps.**

1. Three batches means 3/4 + 3/4 + 3/4, which is 3 × 3/4.
2. Multiply the numerator by the whole number: 3 × 3 = 9, so the product is 9/4.
3. Convert to a mixed number: 9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1, so 9/4 = 2 1/4.
4. 2 1/4 cups of milk are needed.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ How many three-quarters are you putting together?
- _Hint 2:_ 3 × 3/4 means 9 quarter-cups in total. How many whole cups is that?
- _Hint 3:_ Four quarters make one cup. Group your 9 quarters into fours and see what is left over.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                 | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Multiplies the denominator as well.                           | 9/12                 | Ask whether more batches should need more milk or less — 9/12 is smaller than 3/4. |
| Leaves the answer improper when a mixed number was requested. | 9/4                  | Accept the value but ask them to rewrite it as whole cups plus a part.             |
| Converts 9/4 wrongly.                                         | 2 1/2 or 1 1/4       | Ask how many quarters are in 2 whole cups.                                         |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Multiplying only the numerator by the whole number. · Answer: 2 1/4 cups. 9/4 is mathematically correct but does not meet the requested form — mark as incomplete and prompt. · Rubric: R1, R2, R4

## G5-EN-0006 — Dividing a unit fraction by a whole number

**Statement.** Three friends share 1/2 of a pizza equally between them. What fraction of the whole pizza does each friend get?

**Topic:** Fractions · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Divide a fraction by a whole number; Model fraction division with a diagram

**Prerequisite skills:** Sharing as division; Equivalent fractions; Fraction of a whole

**Canonical answer:** 1/6 of the pizza  
**Also accepted:** 1/6, one sixth, 2/12

**Solution steps.**

1. Sharing equally means dividing: 1/2 ÷ 3.
2. Draw the half pizza and cut it into 3 equal pieces.
3. Each of those pieces is a third of a half.
4. If the whole pizza were cut the same way it would have 6 equal pieces, so each piece is 1/6 of the whole pizza.
5. Check: 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Draw the half pizza and split it between the three friends.
- _Hint 2:_ Each friend gets one third of a half. Now think about the whole pizza — how many pieces of that size would it hold?
- _Hint 3:_ Cutting the whole pizza the same way gives 6 equal slices. How many does each friend receive?

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                  | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Multiplies instead of divides. | 3/2 or 1 1/2         | Ask whether each friend can get more than the half they are sharing.                                                   |
| Divides the numerator.         | 1/3                  | Ask whether 1/3 is bigger or smaller than the 1/2 being shared, and whether that makes sense for one person's portion. |
| Subtracts.                     | 1/5 or 2/5           | Re-read the situation together: nothing is being taken away, it is being shared.                                       |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Recognising that a third of a half is a sixth of the whole. · Answer: 1/6 or an equivalent fraction. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0007 — Comparing and ordering decimals

**Statement.** Write these four numbers in order, from smallest to largest: 0.7, 0.07, 0.77, 0.707

**Topic:** Decimals · **Difficulty:** 1 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Compare decimals to thousandths using place value; Order a set of decimals

**Prerequisite skills:** Decimal place value (tenths, hundredths, thousandths); Equivalence such as 0.7 = 0.700

**Canonical answer:** 0.07, 0.7, 0.707, 0.77  
**Also accepted:** 0.07, 0.7, 0.707, 0.77, 0.07 < 0.7 < 0.707 < 0.77

**Solution steps.**

1. Give every number the same number of decimal places: 0.700, 0.070, 0.770, 0.707.
2. Compare the tenths digit first: 0.070 has 0 tenths, so it is the smallest.
3. The other three all have 7 tenths, so compare hundredths: 0.700 has 0, 0.707 has 0, 0.770 has 7 — so 0.77 is the largest of the three.
4. Between 0.700 and 0.707, compare thousandths: 0 < 7, so 0.700 is smaller.
5. Order: 0.07, 0.7, 0.707, 0.77.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ It helps to write every number with the same number of decimal places. What is 0.7 as thousandths?
- _Hint 2:_ Compare the tenths column first, and only move to hundredths when the tenths are equal.
- _Hint 3:_ You have 0.700, 0.070, 0.770 and 0.707. Which one has no tenths at all? That is your smallest.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                                    | Typical wrong answer   | Tutor response                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 'Longer decimal means bigger' — treats digits after the point as a whole number. | 0.07, 0.7, 0.77, 0.707 | Ask the student to compare 0.707 and 0.77 in hundredths: 70 hundredths and a bit, versus 77 hundredths. |
| 'Shorter decimal means bigger'.                                                  | 0.707, 0.77, 0.7, 0.07 | Use a number line between 0 and 1 to place each value.                                                  |
| Ignores the leading zero and reads 0.07 as 0.7.                                  | treats them as equal   | Ask what column the 7 sits in for each number.                                                          |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Comparing digits column by column from the left, not by length. · Answer: Exact sequence 0.07, 0.7, 0.707, 0.77. Reverse order is incorrect — the question specifies smallest first. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0008 — Adding and subtracting decimals in a money context

**Statement.** Leo has $20.00. He buys a book for $7.45 and a pen for $2.80. How much money does he have left?

**Topic:** Decimals · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Add decimals to hundredths; Subtract decimals to hundredths; Solve a two-step word problem

**Prerequisite skills:** Decimal place value; Column addition with carrying; Subtraction with regrouping

**Canonical answer:** $9.75  
**Also accepted:** 9.75, $9.75, 9.75 dollars

**Solution steps.**

1. Find the total spent: 7.45 + 2.80.
2. Line up the decimal points: 7.45 + 2.80 = 10.25.
3. Subtract from what Leo started with: 20.00 − 10.25.
4. 20.00 − 10.25 = 9.75.
5. Leo has $9.75 left.
6. Check: 10.25 + 9.75 = 20.00. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ There are two purchases. What do you need to know before you can subtract?
- _Hint 2:_ First add the two prices together, then take that total away from $20.00.
- _Hint 3:_ He spends $10.25 in total. Write 20.00 − 10.25 in columns with the decimal points lined up.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                               | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Subtracts only one item.                    | 12.55 or 17.20       | Ask how many things Leo bought.                                                 |
| Misaligns decimal points when adding.       | 7.73 or 35.45        | Ask the student to rewrite the sum with the decimal points in a vertical line.  |
| Regrouping error across the zeros in 20.00. | 9.85 or 10.75        | Suggest rewriting 20.00 as 19.100 in working, or adding up from 10.25 to 20.00. |
| Writes the answer as 9.75 with no currency. | 9.75                 | Accept the value and prompt for the money symbol.                               |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Combining both purchases before subtracting. · Answer: 9.75, with currency symbol expected. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3, R4

## G5-EN-0009 — Multiplying a decimal by a whole number

**Statement.** A runner runs 2.75 km every day for 6 days. How far does she run in total?

**Topic:** Decimals · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Multiply a decimal to hundredths by a one-digit whole number; Place the decimal point correctly

**Prerequisite skills:** Multi-digit multiplication; Decimal place value; Estimation for reasonableness

**Canonical answer:** 16.5 km  
**Also accepted:** 16.5, 16.50, 16.5 km, 16 1/2 km

**Solution steps.**

1. Estimate first: 2.75 is close to 3, and 3 × 6 = 18, so the answer should be a little under 18.
2. Ignore the decimal point and multiply: 275 × 6 = 1650.
3. 2.75 has two decimal places, so the answer has two decimal places: 16.50.
4. 16.50 km is usually written as 16.5 km.
5. This matches the estimate of 'a little under 18'. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Roughly how far is it if she ran about 3 km a day for 6 days? Keep that estimate to check your answer.
- _Hint 2:_ Work out 275 × 6 first, then decide where the decimal point belongs.
- _Hint 3:_ 275 × 6 = 1650. There are two digits after the decimal point in 2.75, so count two places back from the right.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                          | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Places the decimal point wrongly.      | 165 or 1.65          | Bring back the estimate: is 165 km a sensible distance for six days of running? |
| Multiplies only the whole-number part. | 12 or 12.75          | Ask whether the 0.75 of a kilometre each day has been counted.                  |
| Adds instead of multiplying.           | 8.75                 | Ask what the 6 is counting.                                                     |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Correct placement of the decimal point, ideally justified by an estimate. · Answer: 16.5 or 16.50, with km. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3, R4

## G5-EN-0010 — Order of operations with brackets

**Statement.** Work out the value of: 5 × (12 − 4) + 18 ÷ 3

**Topic:** Operations and algebraic thinking · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Apply the order of operations; Evaluate an expression containing brackets

**Prerequisite skills:** Multiplication and division facts; Addition and subtraction within 100

**Canonical answer:** 46  
**Also accepted:** 46

**Solution steps.**

1. Brackets first: 12 − 4 = 8, giving 5 × 8 + 18 ÷ 3.
2. Multiplication and division next, working left to right: 5 × 8 = 40 and 18 ÷ 3 = 6.
3. The expression is now 40 + 6.
4. Addition last: 40 + 6 = 46.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Which part of this expression must always be done first?
- _Hint 2:_ Deal with the brackets, then do all the multiplying and dividing, and save the addition for last.
- _Hint 3:_ The expression becomes 5 × 8 + 18 ÷ 3. Work out each of those two products/quotients separately, then add.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                          | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Works strictly left to right.          | ≈24.67               | Ask which operations have priority over addition, and why the brackets are there at all. |
| Ignores the brackets.                  | 62                   | Ask what the brackets are telling them to do first.                                      |
| Divides the whole sum by 3 at the end. | 19 1/3               | Ask which number the ÷ 3 is attached to.                                                 |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Evaluating the bracket before multiplying, and both × and ÷ before the final addition. · Answer: Exact match to 46. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3

## G5-EN-0011 — Area of a composite rectilinear figure

**Statement.** A garden is a rectangle 12 m long and 8 m wide. A rectangular corner of the garden measuring 5 m by 3 m has been paved over. What area of garden is left unpaved?

**Topic:** Measurement and geometry · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Find the area of rectangles; Subtract a part from a whole in a composite figure; Use square units

**Prerequisite skills:** Area = length × width; Multi-digit multiplication; Subtraction

**Canonical answer:** 81 m²  
**Also accepted:** 81, 81 m2, 81 square metres, 81 sq m

**Solution steps.**

1. Find the area of the whole rectangle: 12 × 8 = 96 m².
2. Find the area of the paved corner: 5 × 3 = 15 m².
3. The paved piece sits inside the garden, so subtract it.
4. 96 − 15 = 81.
5. The unpaved garden has an area of 81 m².

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Sketch the garden and shade the paved corner. What two areas do you need?
- _Hint 2:_ Work out the area of the whole rectangle, then the area of the paved rectangle.
- _Hint 3:_ The whole garden is 96 m² and the paving covers 15 m². The paving is part of the garden — so do you add or subtract?

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Forgets the paved section.                   | 96 m²                | Ask whether the paved corner is still garden.                             |
| Adds the two areas.                          | 111 m²               | Ask whether removing paving can make the garden bigger.                   |
| Calculates perimeter instead of area.        | 40 m                 | Ask what the question measures — the space inside or the distance around. |
| Uses square units incorrectly or omits them. | 81 m                 | Prompt for square metres.                                                 |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Subtracting the paved area from the total area. · Answer: 81 with square metres. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3, R4

## G5-EN-0012 — Metric conversion in a multi-step problem

**Statement.** A bag contains 2.4 kg of rice. It is divided into portions of 300 g each. How many full portions can be made?

**Topic:** Measurement and data · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Convert kilograms to grams; Divide to find how many groups; Work with mixed units in one problem

**Prerequisite skills:** Multiplying and dividing by 1000; Decimal place value; Division facts

**Canonical answer:** 8 portions  
**Also accepted:** 8, 8 portions

**Solution steps.**

1. The two masses use different units, so convert first.
2. 1 kg = 1000 g, so 2.4 kg = 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 g.
3. Divide the total by the portion size: 2400 ÷ 300.
4. 2400 ÷ 300 = 8.
5. 8 full portions can be made, with nothing left over.

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ One measurement is in kilograms and the other in grams. What should you do before dividing?
- _Hint 2:_ Convert 2.4 kg into grams by multiplying by 1000, then divide by the portion size.
- _Hint 3:_ 2.4 kg = 2400 g. How many 300 g portions fit into 2400 g? Try thinking of it as 24 ÷ 3.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                          | Typical wrong answer    | Tutor response                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Divides without converting.            | 0.008                   | Ask whether the two numbers are measuring in the same unit.                               |
| Converts using 100 instead of 1000.    | 0.8                     | Ask how many grams are in one kilogram.                                                   |
| Moves the decimal point the wrong way. | 0.0024 kg → tiny answer | Ask whether the number of grams should be larger or smaller than the number of kilograms. |

## G5-EN-0013 — Redistributing data given in fractions

**Statement.** Five beakers hold these amounts of water: 1/2 L, 1/4 L, 3/4 L, 1/2 L and 1/2 L. If all the water is poured into one jug and then shared equally between the five beakers, how much water will be in each beaker?

**Topic:** Measurement and data · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Add fractions with denominators 2 and 4; Divide a total equally; Interpret a levelling-out problem

**Prerequisite skills:** Equivalent fractions; Adding fractions; Sharing as division

**Canonical answer:** 1/2 litre in each beaker  
**Also accepted:** 1/2, 1/2 L, 0.5 L, 2/4 L, half a litre

**Solution steps.**

1. Rewrite every amount in quarters: 2/4, 1/4, 3/4, 2/4, 2/4.
2. Add them: 2 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 10 quarters, so the total is 10/4 L.
3. 10/4 = 2 1/2 litres in the jug.
4. Share between 5 beakers: 2 1/2 ÷ 5, or 10 quarters ÷ 5 = 2 quarters.
5. 2/4 L = 1/2 L, so each beaker holds 1/2 litre.
6. Check: 5 × 1/2 = 2 1/2 L. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Two things need doing here: first find how much water there is altogether.
- _Hint 2:_ Change every amount into quarters so they are easy to add, then divide the total between 5 beakers.
- _Hint 3:_ There are 10 quarter-litres in total. Shared between 5 beakers, how many quarters does each beaker get?

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                                    | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Divides by 4 (the number of different amounts, or the denominator) instead of 5. | 5/8 L                | Ask how many beakers the water is being shared between.                                     |
| Adds numerators and denominators.                                                | 7/16                 | Ask whether the total can be less than the largest single beaker.                           |
| Finds only the total and stops.                                                  | 2 1/2 L              | Confirm the total is right, then ask what the question asks for — the amount in one beaker. |
| Guesses the middle value instead of levelling out.                               | 3/4 L                | Ask them to check: would 5 beakers of 3/4 L use more water than there is?                   |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Totalling first, then dividing by 5. · Answer: 1/2 L or any equivalent; litres expected. · Rubric: R1, R2, R3, R4

## G5-MZ-0001 — Inverse problem on rectangle area with unit conversion

**Statement.** A rectangular plot of land has an area of 24 hectares and a length of 600 metres. Calculate the perimeter of the plot.

**Topic:** Area · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Convert hectares to square metres (1 ha = 10,000 m²); Find the missing side of a rectangle from its area (inverse of Area = length × width); Calculate the perimeter P = 2(length + width)

**Prerequisite skills:** Meaning of area and perimeter as different quantities; Division of a multi-digit number by a round number; Metric units of area

**Canonical answer:** 2000 m (2 km)
**Also accepted:** 2000 m, 2000, 2 km, 2000 metres

**Solution steps.**

1. The area is given in hectares, but the length is in metres, so first convert the area to square metres: 1 ha = 10,000 m², so 24 ha = 24 × 10,000 = 240,000 (m²).
2. The area of a rectangle is A = l × w, so the width w = A ÷ l = 240,000 ÷ 600 = 400 (m).
3. The perimeter of a rectangle is P = 2 × (l + w) = 2 × (600 + 400) = 2 × 1000 = 2000 (m).
4. Answer: 2000 m, or 2 km.
5. Check: 600 × 400 = 240,000 m² = 24 ha. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Pay attention to the units: the area is given in hectares and the length in metres. What should you do first?
- _Hint 2:_ 24 ha = 240,000 m². Knowing the area and the length, how can you find the second side of the rectangle?
- _Hint 3:_ The width is 240,000 ÷ 600 = 400 m. Now remember the formula for the perimeter: how many times does each side appear in it?

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                                  | Typical wrong answer   | Tutor response                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Does not convert hectares at all and divides 24 by 600.                        | 0.04 m → P ≈ 1200.08 m | Ask whether a field 600 m long can be 4 cm wide; then ask what unit the area is measured in. |
| Uses 1 ha = 100 m² or 1000 m².                                                 | P = 1208 m or 1280 m   | Recall that a hectare is a square 100 m × 100 m, so 1 ha = 100 × 100 = 10,000 m².            |
| Stops at the width and reports it as the answer.                               | 400 m                  | Confirm the width is right, then ask what the question actually asks for.                    |
| Adds the sides once, forgetting the factor 2.                                  | 1000 m                 | Ask how many sides a rectangle has and how many of them are 600 m long.                      |
| Calculates area again instead of perimeter, or mixes units (m² in the answer). | 240,000 m²             | Ask which is measured in metres — the boundary or the surface.                               |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Converting 24 ha to 240,000 m² before dividing. · Answer: 2000 m or 2 km. Linear units required; an answer in m² is wrong, not merely unlabelled. · Rubric: Correct final answer; Conversion of units is stated explicitly; Width is found by division, not by guessing; Perimeter formula applied with the factor 2

## G5-MZ-0002 — River current problems

**Statement.** A motorboat's own speed is 27.4 km/h, and the river current is 2.6 km/h. Find the boat's speed with the current and its speed against the current.

**Topic:** Addition and subtraction of decimals · **Difficulty:** 1 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Add and subtract decimals to tenths; Model movement along a river: with the current = own speed + current, against = own speed − current

**Prerequisite skills:** Decimal place value; Column addition and subtraction with aligned decimal points; Meaning of speed

**Canonical answer:** With the current — 30 km/h; against the current — 24.8 km/h
**Also accepted:** 30 km/h and 24.8 km/h, 30.0 and 24.8, 30 km/h with the current; 24.8 km/h against the current

**Solution steps.**

1. The current helps the boat when it goes with it, so the speeds are added.
2. Speed with the current: 27.4 + 2.6 = 30.0 (km/h).
3. The current hinders the boat when it goes against it, so the current speed is subtracted.
4. Speed against the current: 27.4 − 2.6 = 24.8 (km/h).
5. Answer: 24.8 km/h against the current and 30 km/h with the current.
6. Check: the difference between the two speeds is twice the current speed: 30.0 − 24.8 = 5.2 = 2 × 2.6. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Imagine the boat is sailing downstream. Does the river help or hinder it?
- _Hint 2:_ With the current, add the speeds; against the current, subtract them. Write down both operations.
- _Hint 3:_ In columns, decimal point under decimal point: 27.4 + 2.6 and 27.4 − 2.6. Don't forget to include the units.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                             | Typical wrong answer                          | Tutor response                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Swaps the two cases (adds for 'against', subtracts for 'with').           | Against the current 30; with the current 24.8 | Ask which direction is easier to swim in a river, and which answer should therefore be larger. |
| Answers only one of the two questions.                                    | 30 km/h                                       | Point out that the question asks for two speeds.                                               |
| Misaligns the decimal points (adds 2.6 as 26 tenths in the wrong column). | 53.4 or 2.74                                  | Ask the student to rewrite the sum with the decimal points strictly one under the other.       |
| Borrowing error in 27.4 − 2.6.                                            | 26.8 or 25.8                                  | Suggest checking by addition: does the answer plus 2.6 give 27.4?                              |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Correctly assigning addition to 'with the current' and subtraction to 'against'. · Answer: Both values required, each with km/h. One correct value out of two = incomplete, not wrong. · Rubric: Both final answers correct; Reason for adding / subtracting is stated; Units given

## G5-MZ-0003 — Average speed: inverse problem

**Statement.** A bus travelled the first part of a journey in 1.5 hours at a speed of 80 km/h, and the second part in 2.5 hours. At what speed did the bus travel the second part of the journey if the average speed for the entire journey was 67.5 km/h?

**Topic:** Arithmetic mean. Average values · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Understand average speed as total distance ÷ total time (not the mean of the speeds); Multiply and divide decimals; Plan a multi-step solution and work backwards from a total

**Prerequisite skills:** Relationship distance = speed × time; Multiplication of a decimal by a decimal; Division of a decimal by a decimal

**Canonical answer:** 60 km/h
**Also accepted:** 60, 60 km/h

**Solution steps.**

1. Total time: 1.5 + 2.5 = 4.0 (h).
2. Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time, so the total distance is 67.5 × 4.0 = 270 (km).
3. First part of the journey: 80 × 1.5 = 120 (km).
4. Second part of the journey: 270 − 120 = 150 (km).
5. Speed on the second part: 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 (km/h).
6. Check: (120 + 150) ÷ 4.0 = 270 ÷ 4.0 = 67.5 km/h. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ What is average speed: the average of the two speeds, or the total distance divided by the total time?
- _Hint 2:_ First find the total travel time, and from the average speed, find the total distance. What can you find after that?
- _Hint 3:_ The total time is 4.0 h, and the total distance is 67.5 × 4.0 = 270 km. The first part is 80 × 1.5 km. Find the distance for the second part and divide it by 2.5 h.

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                                       | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treats average speed as the arithmetic mean of the two speeds: (80 + v) ÷ 2 = 67.5. | 55 km/h              | Ask whether the two parts took the same time. If not, the slower part weighs more, so the mean of the speeds is not the average speed. Check their value: does it reproduce 67.5? |
| Divides the total distance by 2 instead of subtracting the first part.              | 135 km → 54 km/h     | Ask whether the two parts of the journey are equal in length.                                                                                                                     |
| Forgets to find the total distance and divides 67.5 by 2.5 or similar.              | 27 km/h              | Ask what quantity must be divided by time to give a speed, and whether they have that quantity yet.                                                                               |
| Decimal-point error in 67.5 × 4.0 or 150 ÷ 2.5.                                     | 27.0 km; 6 km/h      | Ask for a rough estimate first: about 70 km/h for about 4 hours is around 280 km.                                                                                                 |
| Answers with the distance instead of the speed.                                     | 150 km               | Confirm the distance is correct, then ask what the question requires.                                                                                                             |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Using average speed = total distance : total time rather than the mean of the two speeds. · Answer: Exactly 60 km/h, with units. · Rubric: Correct final answer; Total time and total distance are computed explicitly; The first part's distance is subtracted, not halved; Units correct throughout

## G5-MZ-0004 — Finding percentages of a number using complement to 100%

**Statement.** $3,600 was spent on renovating the sports hall. 45% of this was paid for labour, and the rest was paid for building materials. How much did the building materials cost?

**Topic:** Percentages. Finding percentages of a number · **Difficulty:** 2 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Find the complementary percentage (100% − p); Find a percentage of a number; Interpret 'the rest' in a word problem

**Prerequisite skills:** Percent as hundredths; Converting a percentage to a decimal; Multiplication and division of a multi-digit number

**Canonical answer:** $1,980 (or currency unit)
**Also accepted:** 1980, 1980 currency units

**Solution steps.**

1. The total amount is 100%. Labour was 45%, so the materials accounted for 100% − 45% = 55%.
2. 55% = 0.55.
3. 3600 × 0.55 = 1980 (currency units).
4. Answer: The building materials cost 1980 currency units.
5. Check: 3600 × 0.45 = 1620 for labour; 1620 + 1980 = 3600. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ What percentage does the total spent amount represent?
- _Hint 2:_ If 45% was paid for labour, what percentage was spent on materials?
- _Hint 3:_ You need to find 55% of 3600. Replace 55% with 0.55 and multiply — or find 1% and multiply by 55.

## G5-MZ-0005 — Inverse percentage problem with complement to 100%

**Statement.** When drying, pears lose 80% of their mass. How many kilograms of fresh pears must be taken to obtain 15 kg of dried pears?

**Topic:** Percentages. Finding a number from its percentage · **Difficulty:** 3 · **Language/grade:** en / Grade 5

**Skills:** Recognise that the dried mass is the complement: 100% − 80% = 20% of the fresh mass; Find a number from a known percentage of it (inverse percentage); Divide by a decimal

**Prerequisite skills:** Percent as hundredths; Distinguishing 'percent of a number' from 'number from its percent'; Division of a decimal by a decimal

**Canonical answer:** 75 kg
**Also accepted:** 75, 75 kg, 75 kilograms

**Solution steps.**

1. The mass of the fresh pears is 100%.
2. If 80% of the mass is lost during drying, then 100% − 80% = 20% of the original mass remains.
3. This 20% is the 15 kg of dried pears.
4. Find the whole from its part: 15 ÷ 0.20 = 75 (kg).
5. Answer: 75 kg of fresh pears must be taken.
6. Check: 75 × 0.20 = 15 kg of dried pears; the loss is 75 − 15 = 60 kg, which is 80% of 75. ✓

**Hint sequence.**

- _Hint 1:_ Pears lose 80% of their mass. What part of the mass remains after drying?
- _Hint 2:_ 20% remains, and this is exactly 15 kg. Do you know the part or the whole? What are you trying to find?
- _Hint 3:_ 15 kg is 20% of the required mass. To find the total mass, divide 15 by 0.20 (or find 1%: 15 ÷ 20, and multiply by 100).

**Common mistakes.**

| Misconception                                                                   | Typical wrong answer | Tutor response                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Uses 80% instead of the remaining 20%.                                          | 15 ÷ 0.80 = 18.75 kg | Ask which part of the fresh mass ends up as dried pears — the 80% that evaporated, or what is left.          |
| Multiplies instead of dividing — finds a percentage of 15 instead of the whole. | 3 kg or 12 kg        | Ask whether fresh pears should weigh more or less than the dried ones. The answer must be bigger than 15 kg. |
| Adds 80% of 15 to 15.                                                           | 27 kg                | Ask what the 80% is a percentage of — the fresh mass or the dried mass.                                      |
| Decimal-point error when dividing by 0.20.                                      | 7.5 kg or 750 kg     | Estimate together: 20% is one fifth, so the answer should be roughly five times 15.                          |

**Checking criteria.** Key step: Identifying 15 kg as 20% (not 80%) of the unknown mass, then dividing. · Answer: 75 kg exactly, with units. Any answer smaller than 15 kg should be treated as a modelling error, not an arithmetic slip. · Rubric: Correct final answer; The 100% − 80% = 20% step is explicit; Correct choice of division over multiplication, ideally justified by 'the answer must exceed 15 kg'; Units given

**Source:** Adapted from Merzlyak A. G. et al. Collection of Problems..., Grade 5. — Kh.: Gimnazia, 2010. — Variant 1, No. 247, p. 30. · **Licence:** Original content — free to use, modify and distribute within the project. · **Review status:** `draft`
